identifier, a table's rows one to a line, `raco fhir-mcp --verbs query,describe` for the MCP arm,

**What the remaining misses are**
- MedAgentBench: three tasks (task5_21, task5_25, task9_3) are missed by every arm, plain REST
  included. The MCP arm's other ten are one thing: task 8's ServiceRequest written with `note` as
  a list of Annotations, which is what FHIR defines, where the benchmark's grader reads an object.
- FHIR-AgentBench: 26 of the workspace arm's 32 misses are missed by all three arms. 15 have "no
  answer" as their truth where the agents answered; 11 of those ask about "this" or the "current"
  hospital visit, which the agents read as the stay open at the question's time and the benchmark
  answers as none. The others are the benchmark's choice among records the question's words fit:
  the time a prescription starts where the agents give the time it was ordered, a vital sign or
  lab from another source than the one found, an organism or specimen of another test, a count or
  a "first" among orders written at the same moment. The six misses that are not every arm's
  change from run to run.

**Where the tokens are**
- A turn costs about 11k tokens of context, and a question takes its data calls plus two turns
  (one writes the answer, one closes): 69 of the 150 questions took one data call and cost 34k;
  34 took two (50k); the 12 that took six or more cost 143k each, a fifth of the whole run.
- Second calls are orientation: which code a name means (`find`), which encounter "this visit" is
  (`as=summary`, an Encounter search), then the search itself. Agents already send two calls in one
  turn with `;`. What would remove a turn is a query that says both steps, which is what CQL and
  SQL are for; agents used SQL 22 times in 150 questions.
- The closing turn is the harness's, the same for every arm: an answer read from the agent's last
  message would save one turn in three.
